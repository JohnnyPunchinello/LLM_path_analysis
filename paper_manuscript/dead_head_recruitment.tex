\begin{abstract}
Expressivity theory tells us what a transformer \emph{can} compute: fixed
depth bounds the number of \emph{sequential} steps available, while heads
supply \emph{parallel} capacity. We ask the complementary empirical question:
given a trained model and a task, how much of each resource does it actually
\emph{allocate}? We characterise every task by two structural coordinates of
its dependency graph---serial depth $D$ (the critical path) and parallel
width $W$ (the largest set of mutually independent intermediate values)---and
every prediction by two allocation measures read off the network's
active-head landscape: recruited width $\Wh$ (active heads per layer) and
recruited depth $\Dh$ (the active frontier). Our organising hypothesis is a
\emph{resource-matching law}: $\Dh$ should track $D$ and $\Wh$ should track
$W$, with negligible cross-terms, at exchange rates $c$ (layers per serial
step) and $\gamma$ (heads per parallel item) that in turn predict a capability
ceiling $D_{\max}\approx L_{\text{eff}}/c$. We test this with a factorial
suite that varies $D$ and $W$ independently under a length control.
On Llama-3-70B the width half holds cleanly---parallel width recruits heads
($r{=}{+}0.81$ length-matched; grid fit $\Wh \approx 32.3-0.14\,D+0.25\,W$)
while serial depth recruits none---and the length control proves necessary,
since the uncontrolled ladder trends the wrong way. The depth half is null on
this model, which the framework explains as the expected \emph{sub-ceiling}
regime: a 70B idles ${\sim}50\%$ of its heads, so a six-step task never
approaches its serial budget. In lower-reserve models the depth axis does
appear (Llama-3-8B: $r(\text{hops},\text{frontier}){=}{+}0.96$; pooled
$r{=}{+}0.70$ across the largest GPT-2/Pythia models), and recruitment
overall scales with model capacity. We then show how architecture sets the
two budgets---depth $L$, heads $H$, KV groups $n_{kv}$ under grouped-query
attention, experts under mixture-of-experts, and the aspect ratio $L/H$---and
derive falsifiable cross-architecture predictions. Finally we delimit what
active-node geometry does and does not reveal about circuits: it constrains a
circuit's support, width and depth-extent, but not its wiring; combined with a
known task graph it identifies the \emph{embedding} of that graph into the
layer$\times$head lattice, which is the sense in which task structure can be
read from activation structure. Complete data (84 measurements) and an
experimental roadmap are included.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ======================================================================
Residual transformers expose, at every layer, two routes for information: a
\emph{compute path} through the attention heads and the feed-forward (FFN)
block, and an \emph{identity residual path} that bypasses them. A large
body of work establishes that the compute path is heavily underused---most
attention heads can be pruned with little loss
\citep{michel2019sixteen,voita2019analyzing}, deep layers are frequently
redundant \citep{men2024shortgpt,gromov2024unreasonable}, and harder inputs
deserve more computation \citep{graves2016adaptive,schuster2022confident}.
What is missing is a \emph{graded, per-task} account: not ``which heads are
removable in general,'' but \textbf{how the set of working heads grows as a
task becomes harder, and how that growth is bounded by model capacity.}

A parallel theoretical literature makes the resource structure precise.
Fixed-depth log-precision transformers occupy a shallow parallel class with a
\emph{bounded number of sequential steps} \citep{merrill2023parallelism};
chain-of-thought tokens are how a model buys serial steps it cannot fit in
depth \citep{merrill2024cot,li2024chain,feng2023towards}; and the dichotomy is
explicit in expressivity results showing that parallel-style problems are
solvable by one wide layer while $k$-hop and pointer-chasing problems
\emph{provably require depth} \citep{sanford2023representational,%
sanford2024transformers}. These are statements about what an architecture
\emph{can} compute in the worst case. They leave open the empirical question
this paper addresses:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Given a \textbf{trained} model and a specific task, how much serial
(depth) and parallel (head) resource does it actually allocate---and does the
allocation match the task's own serial and parallel structure?}
\end{quote}

We answer by giving both sides of that sentence a measurable definition. On
the task side, every task carries two structural coordinates of its dependency
graph: \emph{serial depth} $D$ (the critical path) and \emph{parallel width}
$W$ (the largest set of mutually independent intermediate values). On the
network side, every prediction induces an active-head set whose geometry gives
\emph{recruited depth} $\Dh$ (how deep the active region reaches) and
\emph{recruited width} $\Wh$ (how many heads per layer are engaged). The
organising hypothesis is a \textbf{resource-matching law}: $\Dh$ tracks $D$,
$\Wh$ tracks $W$, and the cross-terms vanish (Section~\ref{sec:framework}).

Two consequences make this more than a restatement. First, the diagonal
coefficients are \emph{exchange rates with units}---$c$ layers per serial
step, $\gamma$ heads per parallel item---and $c$ predicts a capability ceiling
$D_{\max}\approx L_{\text{eff}}/c$ from architecture alone. Second, the
budgets are set by architectural choices ($L$, $H$, KV groups, experts, aspect
ratio $L/H$), so the law generates falsifiable cross-architecture predictions
(Section~\ref{sec:arch}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{A framework} that makes task complexity and network allocation
commensurable: definitions of $(D,W)$ and $(\Dh,\Wh)$, the resource-matching
law with its exchange rates, and the capacity ceiling it implies
(Section~\ref{sec:framework}).
\item \textbf{A controlled $(D,W)$ factorial} that varies serial depth and
parallel width independently under a length control, establishing on
Llama-3-70B that \emph{parallel width recruits heads and serial depth does
not} (Section~\ref{sec:factorial}).
\item \textbf{Supporting allocation results}: demand-graded recruitment, its
dilation with model scale, and frontier extension with reasoning depth in
lower-reserve models (Sections~\ref{sec:recruit}--\ref{sec:frontier}).
\item \textbf{An architectural account} of how $L$, $H$, $n_{kv}$, experts and
aspect ratio set the two budgets, with cross-architecture predictions
(Section~\ref{sec:arch}).
\item \textbf{A delimitation of what active-node geometry says about
circuits}---support, width and depth-extent, but not wiring---and the sense in
which task structure is recoverable from activation structure
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
\item The complete 84-measurement dataset (Appendix~\ref{app:data}) and an
experimental roadmap (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
% ======================================================================
\paragraph{Attention-head and layer redundancy.}
\citet{michel2019sixteen} showed that a large fraction of attention heads can
be pruned at test time with negligible loss, and that importance is highly
uneven across heads. \citet{voita2019analyzing} reached a similar conclusion
for encoder self-attention, identifying a small set of specialised heads
(positional, syntactic, rare-token) that do most of the work while the rest
are removable. At the layer level, \citet{men2024shortgpt} introduce a
\emph{Block Influence} score---the degree to which a block changes the hidden
state---and find many deep layers contribute little; \citet{gromov2024unreasonable}
likewise prune large contiguous spans of late layers with small degradation.
\citet{fan2020reducing} (LayerDrop) and \citet{sajjad2023effect} study
structured layer dropping during and after training. \emph{All of this work
treats redundancy as a (largely) static property of a trained network. Our
contribution is to make redundancy a measured, monotone function of task
demand---the dead set is not fixed but is drawn down as the task gets
harder.}

\paragraph{Conditional and adaptive computation.}
\citet{graves2016adaptive} introduced Adaptive Computation Time, letting a
recurrent model learn how many steps to spend per input. The idea was carried
into transformers by Universal Transformers \citep{dehghani2019universal} and
by depth-adaptive / early-exit methods
\citep{elbayad2020depth,xin2020deebert,schuster2022confident}, which halt
computation once a per-token confidence criterion is met.
\citet{raposo2024mixture} (Mixture-of-Depths) route only some tokens through
each block. \emph{These methods adapt essentially one scalar---how deep to
go. We provide evidence for a second, orthogonal axis the model already uses
internally: how \emph{wide} (how many heads) to engage at each layer, as a
graded function of demand.}

\paragraph{Reasoning, multi-hop recall, and serial computation.}
A complementary line studies where multi-step computations occur.
\citet{geva2023dissecting} and \citet{meng2022locating} localise factual
recall to specific layers and modules; \citet{yang2024large} show that
multi-hop reasoning is performed latently across successive layer ranges, and
\citet{biran2024hopping} show that later hops in a chain resolve in later
layers and can fail when there is insufficient depth; \citet{brinkmann2024mechanistic}
and \citet{stolfo2023mechanistic} trace multi-step symbolic and arithmetic
computation across layers. \emph{These works motivate using a serial-reasoning
ladder as a clean complexity axis; we measure how the internal active-head set
responds to it.}

\paragraph{Expressivity: depth is the serial budget, heads the parallel one.}
The resource structure we measure is established theoretically.
\citet{merrill2023parallelism} show fixed-depth log-precision transformers lie
in a shallow parallel class---unbounded fan-in but a \emph{bounded} number of
sequential steps---and \citet{merrill2024cot} show that chain-of-thought
decoding steps buy exactly the serial computation depth cannot supply
(logarithmically many steps stay within $\mathsf{L}$, polynomially many
capture $\mathsf{P}$); \citet{li2024chain} and \citet{feng2023towards} reach
the same conclusion from complementary angles. Most directly,
\citet{sanford2023representational,sanford2024transformers} prove the
dichotomy we test: parallel-style problems (e.g.\ sparse averaging) are
solvable by a single sufficiently wide layer, whereas $k$-hop induction and
pointer chasing \emph{require} depth growing with the hop count. \emph{These
are worst-case statements about what an architecture can compute. Our
contribution is orthogonal and empirical: we measure what a trained model
allocates, per task, and whether the allocation matches the task's $(D,W)$
structure.}

\paragraph{Shortcuts: models need not execute the serial algorithm.}
\citet{liu2023shortcuts} show transformers frequently learn shallow, parallel
\emph{shortcuts} to problems whose reference algorithm is serial, computing in
far fewer sequential steps than the task nominally requires. This is the main
reason a formally $D$-deep task may recruit no additional depth, and it makes
accuracy measurement indispensable: without it, a flat depth response is
ambiguous between ``inside the budget'', ``shortcut'' and ``failed''. We
incorporate this as an explicit regime of the framework
(Section~\ref{sec:framework}) rather than as noise.

\paragraph{Stages of inference and residual-stream analysis.}
\citet{lad2024remarkable} argue that inference proceeds through a small number
of qualitatively distinct stages (detokenisation, feature construction,
prediction, sharpening), which is consistent with the conserved structure we
observe (Section~\ref{sec:skeleton}). The logit lens
\citep{nostalgebraist2020logitlens} and tuned lens
\citep{belrose2023eliciting} read out the \emph{content} of the residual
stream by depth. \emph{We instead study the \emph{routing}: which compute
units carry attribution, and how their number changes with task demand.}

\paragraph{Circuits and attribution.}
Mechanistic circuit work names specific components for specific tasks: a
mathematical framework for the residual stream \citep{elhage2021mathematical},
the indirect-object-identification circuit \citep{wang2022interpretability},
and automated circuit discovery \citep{conmy2023towards} built on attribution
patching \citep{nanda2023attribution}; \citet{ferrando2024information} trace
information-flow routes at scale. \emph{We use the same per-head attribution
criterion, but study the aggregate complement---the dead set---across a graded
task family, a level of description above any single circuit.}

\section{Framework: task complexity and network allocation}\label{sec:framework}
% ======================================================================
The framework has three parts: two coordinates describing the \emph{task}, two
describing the \emph{network's response}, and a law relating them.

\subsection{Task side: serial depth $D$ and parallel width $W$}
Let $G_t$ be the minimal dependency graph of a task $t$: a DAG whose nodes are
the intermediate values needed to map the prompt to the answer, with an edge
$u\!\to\!v$ when computing $v$ requires $u$. Define
\begin{align}
D(t) &= \text{length of the longest directed path in } G_t
        &&\text{(\emph{serial depth}: the critical path)},\\
W(t) &= \text{size of the largest antichain in } G_t
        &&\text{(\emph{parallel width}: mutually independent values)} .
\end{align}
$D$ counts steps that \emph{must} happen in sequence; $W$ counts values that
must be held \emph{simultaneously}. Both are properties of the task
specification, not of any model, and in our synthetic suites both are fixed by
construction (Section~\ref{sec:method}): a $k$-step pointer chase has
$D{=}k,W{=}1$; retrieving one of $m$ independent bindings has $D{=}1,W{=}m$.

\subsection{Network side: recruited depth $\Dh$ and width $\Wh$}
For a model with $L$ layers and $H$ heads per layer, let $s(\ell,h)$ be a
per-head activity score (Section~\ref{sec:method}) and call a head \emph{dead}
when $s(\ell,h) < \tau\max_{h'}s(\ell,h')$; the complement is the
\emph{active set} $A(p)\subseteq[L]\times[H]$, and $\dl$ is the per-layer dead
count. Write $u=\ell/(L{-}1)\in[0,1]$ for normalised depth. Define
\begin{align}
\Wh(p) &= \tfrac{1}{L}\textstyle\sum_{\ell}\big(H-\dl\big)
   &&\text{(\emph{recruited width}: active heads per layer)},\\
\Dh(p) &= q_{25}\ \text{of the dead-mass distribution over } u
   &&\text{(\emph{recruited depth}: the active frontier)} .
\end{align}
$\Dh$ is a front-edge quantile---the depth by which the first quarter of dead
mass has accumulated---because the \emph{back} of the network holds a
task-invariant sparse readout region that pins the centroid and makes it blind
to recruitment at the front. For cross-architecture comparison we always also
report the normalised forms $\Wh/H$ and $\Dh$ (already a depth fraction);
absolute head counts are not comparable between models with different $H$.

\subsection{The resource-matching law}
The hypothesis is that the model's allocation matches the task's structure
axis-by-axis:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \Dh \;=\; a + c\,D + c'\,W, \qquad
        \Wh \;=\; b + \gamma\,W + \gamma'\,D\ }
\label{eq:law}
\end{equation}
with the \textbf{dissociation prediction} $c'\!\approx\!0$ and
$\gamma'\!\approx\!0$: serial demand buys layers but not heads, parallel demand
buys heads but not layers. The coefficients carry meaning beyond significance:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item $c$ = \textbf{layers per serial step} and $\gamma$ = \textbf{heads per
parallel item}: exchange rates converting task structure into network
resource.
\item $a,b$ = the \textbf{task-invariant scaffold} recruited regardless of
demand (detokenisation, readout).
\item $c',\gamma'$ = \textbf{leakage} between axes. A large $\gamma'$ means the
model \emph{parallelises} a serial task (a shortcut signature); a large $c'$
means it \emph{serialises} a parallel task (width-to-depth conversion under
capacity pressure). Both are findings, not noise.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Capacity: feasibility and the ceiling}
The lattice is finite, so allocation is bounded:
\begin{equation}
c\,D \;\le\; L_{\text{eff}}, \qquad \gamma\,W \;\le\; H,
\label{eq:feasible}
\end{equation}
where $L_{\text{eff}} = L - (\text{readout stages})$ is the usable serial
budget. Equation~\eqref{eq:feasible} yields the framework's sharpest
prediction---a capability ceiling derived from architecture alone:
\begin{equation}
D_{\max} \;\approx\; L_{\text{eff}}\,/\,c .
\label{eq:dmax}
\end{equation}

Because depth is a \emph{bounded budget} rather than a linear resource
\citep{merrill2023parallelism}, the expected shape of $\Dh(D)$ is not a line
but flat-then-saturating: allocation absorbs serial steps until the budget
binds. We therefore fit Eq.~\eqref{eq:law} as a \emph{null model} and read its
deviations, which---combined with accuracy---separate three regimes:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Regime & Allocation $\Dh$ & Accuracy & Interpretation \\
\midrule
R1 sub-ceiling ($cD\!\ll\!L_{\text{eff}}$) & rises with $D$ (or flat if slack) & high & inside the serial budget \\
R2 shortcut & flat & high & model did not compute serially \citep{liu2023shortcuts} \\
R3 beyond ceiling & saturated & collapses & serial budget exhausted \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Accuracy is what makes R1--R3 distinguishable; a flat $\Dh$ alone is
uninterpretable. This directly explains our Llama-3-70B result
(Section~\ref{sec:factorial}): with $L{=}80$ and a six-step ladder, the model
is deep in R1, where the framework predicts little or no depth response.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
% ======================================================================
\paragraph{Dead heads.}
For a prompt $p$ we compute, for each attention head $(\ell,h)$, a scalar
attribution score $s(\ell,h)$ via gradient$\,\times\,$activation on the head's
output (the per-head attribution of \texttt{compute\_per\_head\_scores} in the
analysis pipeline). A head is \emph{dead} for $p$ iff
\[
s(\ell,h) \;<\; \tau \cdot \max_{h'} s(\ell,h'), \qquad \tau = 0.15,
\]
i.e.\ it carries less than $15\%$ of the attribution of the strongest head in
its own layer. The \emph{dead-head landscape} is
$\dl = \#\{\,h : (\ell,h)\text{ dead}\,\}$, and the total dead count is
$\sum_\ell \dl$.

\paragraph{Models.}
Six pretrained models spanning two families and a range of depth/width:
GPT-2 medium ($24\text{L}\times16\text{H}$), large ($36\times20$), xl
($48\times25$); Pythia-70M ($6\times8$), 160M ($12\times12$), 410M
($24\times16$).

\paragraph{Task ladders.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{deep\_chains} --- a \emph{pure serial-reasoning ladder}:
``\texttt{A is the parent of B. B is the parent of C.\ \ldots\ A's $k$-th
descendant is}'' for $k=1\ldots6$. Each rung adds exactly one composition
step; the surface template is held fixed, isolating reasoning depth.
\item \textbf{complexity\_gradient} --- eight heterogeneous tasks ordered by
nominal difficulty: lexical, subject--verb agreement, 1-hop factual,
arithmetic, 2-hop, 3-hop, syllogism, analogy. Its 1/2/3-hop reasoning subset
is a secondary controlled axis.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Measurements.}
For every (model, task) chart we report total dead heads; the dead fraction
$\sum_\ell\dl / (L\cdot H)$; the dead-mass \emph{centroid} in fractional depth
($0=$ first layer, $1=$ last); the fraction of dead mass in the front half of
the network; and the \emph{front-edge depth} $q_{25}$---the normalised depth
at which the first quarter of dead mass accumulates. $q_{25}$ is our primary
horizontal-axis statistic because, unlike the centroid, it is not dominated by
the task-invariant sparse readout region at the back of the network and so is
sensitive to where the \emph{active frontier} sits. The complete set of
$6\times8 + 6\times6 = 84$ measurements is listed in Appendix~\ref{app:data}.

\paragraph{Auxiliary exact data.}
To corroborate the figure-recovered trends with ground-truth attribution we
additionally analyse one model, Llama-3-8B ($32\text{L}\times32\text{H}$), for
which exact per-head dead masks are available from the analysis pipeline (read
directly from the per-head graph files, not recovered from a rendered chart).
We use it only as a high-fidelity check on the two-axis correlations.

\paragraph{Data provenance and recovery.}
The sweep was archived as rendered per-layer bar charts (the
\texttt{abdulla\_results/} directory; subfolders \texttt{graphs},
\texttt{graphs~2}, \texttt{graphs~3}). Because the underlying numeric arrays
were not saved, we recover per-layer counts directly from the images with a
calibrated extractor (\texttt{extract\_dead\_heads.py}): the $y$-axis is
calibrated from the rendered ``total heads $=N$'' line and the plot baseline,
and each layer's bar height is read as a contiguous run anchored at the
baseline. Recovered values were validated against the printed bar labels on
GPT-2~xl and GPT-2~large to within $\pm1$--$2$ heads. We treat the recovered
data as reliable for trend analysis; regenerating exact arrays from the
pipeline is the first item on the roadmap (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}).

demand}\label{sec:frontier}

depth the recruited heads come from by tracking the front edge of the
dead-head distribution---the normalised depth $q_{25}$ at which the first
quarter of dead mass has accumulated (a front-sensitive statistic, unlike the
centroid, which is anchored by the task-invariant sparse readout region at the
back). As reasoning depth rises, $q_{25}$ moves deeper: the dead zone retreats
and the \emph{active frontier} advances. Table~\ref{tab:twoaxis} reports both
axes together as correlations with hop count.

The signal is clearest in the model with exact (non-recovered) attribution.
On Llama-3-8B the three-rung reasoning ladder shows total dead falling
($425\to256\to248$), early-layer ($\ell\!\le\!10$) dead falling sharply
($92\to47\to42$), and the frontier advancing monotonically
($q_{25}: 0.46\to0.54\to0.58$; centroid $0.67\to0.71\to0.76$). Among the
figure-recovered models the same coupling holds for the three largest
(GPT-2~xl/large, Pythia-410M): $r(\text{hops},q_{25})$ pools to $+0.70$
($n{=}18$). The three smallest models do not show it---their counts are
quantisation-noisy and, with only $6$--$24$ layers, they appear to exhaust
their reserve within one or two hops, so neither axis has room to move. This
is the same scale dependence seen for recruitment (Section~\ref{sec:scale}):
both axes require spare capacity to be visible.

\paragraph{A per-hop depth cost.}
Converting the frontier shift to absolute layers gives a serial cost per
reasoning step: $\approx1.9$ layers/hop on Llama-3-8B (1$\to$3 hop), and
$0.3$--$0.5$ layers/hop on the larger GPT-2/Pythia chains. The smaller
per-hop cost on the GPT-2 chains is consistent with early saturation---the
frontier stops advancing once the model can no longer carry out additional
hops---which predicts that the frontier should track task \emph{accuracy}, a
test we make explicit in the roadmap (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}). This
per-hop layer cost is the empirical, per-layer counterpart of the theoretical
result that transformer depth bounds serial computation
\citep{merrill2023parallelism,li2024chain} and the finding that later hops
resolve in later layers and fail without sufficient depth
\citep{yang2024large,biran2024hopping}.

\subsection{A conserved backbone}\label{sec:skeleton}
The recruitment that does occur is a deformation of a stable structure, not a
redrawing of it. Most dead mass sits in the back half of the network at every
difficulty level (front-half fractions are small and roughly constant; see
Appendix~\ref{app:data}): the late layers keep attention sparse regardless of
task, while recruitment is concentrated in the early-to-middle band. This
conserved-then-deformed picture is consistent with the view that inference
proceeds through a small set of qualitatively distinct stages
\citep{lad2024remarkable}.

\subsection{Controlled dissociation: a $(D,W)$ factorial}\label{sec:factorial}
The complexity gradient and deep chains confound \emph{what} kind of demand
rises with \emph{how much}. To test the sharper hypothesis---that
\emph{serial} demand (dependency depth $D$) recruits network \emph{depth}
while \emph{parallel} demand (independent width $W$) recruits attention
\emph{heads} (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic})---we built a factorial task suite
that varies $D$ and $W$ independently: a pure-serial ladder (a constant-length
pointer-chase, $D{=}1\ldots6$, $W{=}1$), a pure-parallel ladder (find a unique
binding among $W$ items, $D{=}1$, $W{=}1\ldots6$, with a length-matched
variant), and a crossed $3{\times}3$ grid. We measure, per task, the recruited
\emph{width} $\hat W$ (active heads per layer) and recruited \emph{depth}
$\hat D$ (the active-frontier depth, i.e.\ the layer quantiles of the
dead-mass distribution). Results are on Llama-3-70B\footnote{Meta-Llama-3-70B,
loaded in bfloat16 with CPU offload; forward-only magnitude metric.} and should
be read as a proof-of-method: the effects are small because, as
Section~\ref{sec:scale} shows, a 70B holds an enormous idle reserve that
compresses all recruitment.

\paragraph{Width recruits heads; depth does not.}
On the length-matched parallel ladder, active heads per layer rise with $W$
($r{=}{+}0.81$; Figure~\ref{fig:axes} left), while the active frontier does not
move ($r(W, q_{25}){=}{-}0.09$). The crossed grid confirms and separates the
two axes: fitting active-heads-per-layer gives
$\approx 32.3 - 0.14\,D + 0.25\,W$---a clean positive width coefficient and a
near-zero depth coefficient (Figure~\ref{fig:grid})---while the depth
coefficient on the frontier is $\approx 0.0005$ (in depth fraction), i.e.\
zero. \textbf{Parallel width engages more heads at fixed depth; serial depth
does not engage more heads.} This is the width half of the predicted double
dissociation.

\paragraph{The length control is necessary.}
The \emph{raw} parallel ladder shows active heads \emph{falling} with $W$---the
opposite of the prediction---but this reverses once prompt length is held
constant (Figure~\ref{fig:axes} left, dashed vs.\ solid). The raw trend is a
length artifact (a longer prompt leaves more heads below threshold at the final
token); only the length-matched ladder reveals the true width effect. We
therefore treat length matching as mandatory for the width axis.

\paragraph{The depth axis is not resolved on the 70B (a null, not a
refutation).}
Serial depth produces \emph{no} detectable frontier extension here: across
$D{=}1\ldots6$ the front-edge, centroid, and back-edge of the active region
each shift by less than one layer on an 80-layer network
(Figure~\ref{fig:axes} right)---noise. We read this as an instrument problem,
not evidence against the hypothesis. A 70B idles $\sim$half its heads
(Section~\ref{sec:scale}), so a six-step task is trivially within capacity and
barely perturbs the geometry; and without accuracy measurements we cannot
verify the model computes the pointer-chase serially at all. The depth axis
must be tested where the reserve is small---the GPT-2/Pythia models, on which
six hops is a large fraction of total depth---with per-cell accuracy and
paraphrase replicates. We state this explicitly so the confirmed width axis is
not over-read into a full dissociation.

\paragraph{Predictions.}
The framework converts these differences into falsifiable statements. We state
them as pre-registered predictions; the runs that test them are itemised in
Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.9em,itemsep=3pt,label=\textbf{A\arabic*.}]
\item \textbf{The parallel budget is head-structured.} $\gamma$ (heads per
parallel item) should scale with the number of \emph{independent} attention
channels. Under multi-head attention that is $H$; under grouped-query
attention, where many query heads share one KV group, the effective budget may
be closer to $n_{kv}$. Prediction: width recruitment saturates near $n_{kv}$,
not $H$, in GQA models.
\item \textbf{The serial budget is depth.} Either $c$ is roughly constant in
\emph{absolute layers} across models (so deeper models simply fit more steps),
or constant as a \emph{fraction} of $L$ (so all models exhaust their budget at
the same $D$). These give very different $D_{\max}$ predictions via
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmax}, and discriminating them is the central cross-model
measurement.
\item \textbf{Block topology should not matter for the depth axis.} Pythia
computes attention and MLP \emph{in parallel} within a block, GPT-2
sequentially. At matched $(L,H)$---Pythia-410M vs.\ GPT-2-medium, both
$24{\times}16$---the framework predicts the same $c$, i.e.\ the depth axis is a
property of layer count rather than intra-block wiring.
\item \textbf{Aspect ratio beats parameter count for serial tasks.} Pythia-1B
($16{\times}8$) is \emph{shallower and narrower} than Pythia-410M
($24{\times}16$) despite having more parameters. Prediction: 1B hits its serial
ceiling \emph{earlier} than 410M---a within-family dissociation of capability
from scale.
\item \textbf{The width axis is attention-specific; the depth axis is not.}
State-space models have layers but no heads. Prediction: the depth axis
survives in Mamba-2/RWKV (serial demand still recruits layers) while the width
axis has no attentional analogue---separating the two axes by architecture
rather than by task. Correspondingly, in mixture-of-experts models the width
axis should extend into \emph{expert} space (parallel demand recruits more
experts per layer).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What the present data can and cannot say.}
Our measurements span two of these cells and are suggestive rather than
decisive. (i) Both GPT-2 (sequential) and Pythia (parallel-block) families show
demand-graded recruitment and, in their larger members, frontier extension
(Sections~\ref{sec:recruit},~\ref{sec:frontier}), consistent with A3 but not a
matched-$(L,H)$ test. (ii) The Llama-3 models are the only GQA systems here,
and their unusually large idle fraction ($\sim$50\% of \emph{query} heads
inactive on the 70B under forward-only metrics) is consistent with an effective
parallel budget nearer $n_{kv}{=}8$ than $H{=}64$---but this is a
single-architecture observation, confounded with scale, and we flag it as a
hypothesis for A1 rather than evidence for it. (iii) No SSM or MoE model was
measured. A1--A5 are therefore predictions of the framework, not results of
this paper.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
% ======================================================================
The measurements support a compact two-axis account. Treat each model as
holding a reserve of dormant attention heads that it \emph{recruits} on
demand.

\emph{Vertical axis (recruitment).} Rising task demand---most cleanly,
additional reasoning steps---is paid for by activating more of this reserve,
so $\dl$ deflates monotonically. The size of the reserve, and how much of it
is mobilised, are set by model capacity: larger models hold more idle width
and mobilise more of it under load.

\emph{Horizontal axis (frontier extension).} The recruitment is not
spatially uniform. Each additional composition step pulls the active frontier
deeper into the network: early and middle layers that idle on a shallow task
are engaged on a deeper one, and the front edge of the dead zone retreats
(Section~\ref{sec:frontier}). This advance has an estimable per-hop layer
cost and appears to plateau when the model runs out of usable depth, linking
the geometry directly to the serial-depth limits of transformers
\citep{merrill2023parallelism,yang2024large,biran2024hopping}.

The two axes are coupled---more demand both deflates the landscape and
advances its frontier---and both are gated by spare capacity, which is why
they emerge in the larger models and vanish into noise in the smallest. The
deformation acts on a conserved backbone: a task-invariant sparse readout
region at the back persists at every difficulty, while the action happens in
the early-to-middle band. In short: a capacity-gated recruitment response with
a magnitude (how many heads) and a reach (how deep).

\subsection{What active-node geometry says about circuit structure}
\label{sec:circuits}
A circuit is a set of \emph{paths}---head reading from head, composing into a
readout. Our observable is a set of \emph{nodes}. The relationship is a
marginal, and being precise about it determines what may be claimed.

\paragraph{What node activity licenses.}
Under the faithfulness assumption that dead units are dispensable (the
ablation check in Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}, item~D$'$), the active set
\emph{contains} the circuit's nodes. Node activity therefore constrains:
(i) the circuit's \textbf{support}---an over-approximation of where it lives;
(ii) its \textbf{geometry}---width per layer, depth-extent, total size; and
(iii) its \textbf{schedule}---which layers are working at all. In the
path-bundle picture, $\Wh$ is the bundle's layer-wise width and $\Dh$ is how
deep the bundle reaches, which is exactly why $(D,W)$ maps onto $(\Dh,\Wh)$:
serial structure lengthens the bundle, parallel structure widens it.

\paragraph{What it does not license.}
Node activity is silent about \textbf{wiring}. Two circuits with different
connectivity can activate identical nodes, so we cannot say from $\dl$ which
active head reads from which earlier head. Edge-level methods are required for
that: path patching \citep{goldowsky2023localizing}, automated circuit
discovery \citep{conmy2023towards,syed2023attribution}, and information-flow
routes \citep{ferrando2024information}, of which our node counts are a cheap
marginal. We therefore make no wiring claims.

\paragraph{The task prior is what closes the gap.}
Node geometry alone underdetermines a circuit---but we never have it alone. We
have it \emph{paired with a task whose computational structure we designed}.
Nodes plus a known $G_t$ identify the \textbf{embedding} of $G_t$ into the
layer$\times$head lattice: if a $k$-hop task is known to require $k$ sequential
steps and we observe $k$ successive active bands, the task prior licenses the
inference that each band carries one hop---an inferred \emph{schedule}, even
though no edge was measured. This is the precise sense in which task structure
is readable from activation structure, and it is weaker than circuit discovery
by design.

\paragraph{A two-way correspondence.}
The framework is thus a correspondence between task structure and active-region
geometry, usable in both directions. \emph{Forward}: $(D,W)$ predicts
$(\Dh,\Wh)$---the law of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law}. \emph{Inverse}: the geometry of
the active region decodes the task's computational type, making the active
subgraph a \textbf{fingerprint of the computation}. The inverse direction is
attractive precisely because it requires no wiring claim: showing that a simple
decoder recovers $(D,W)$ from activation geometry demonstrates that node
activity \emph{carries} structural information about the computation, whatever
the wiring turns out to be.

\paragraph{Relation to circuit discovery.}
The two approaches are complementary rather than competing. Circuit discovery
recovers wiring for \emph{one} task at high cost; we recover geometry for
\emph{many} tasks cheaply, and---because the task family is graded---obtain a
scaling law that single-circuit studies cannot express. Most mechanistic work
asks ``what is the circuit for task $X$''; we ask ``how does circuit
\emph{shape} vary with the structure of $X$''. Studies of circuit reuse
\citep{merullo2024circuit}, function vectors \citep{todd2024function}, and
specialised head families---induction \citep{olsson2022induction}, retrieval
\citep{wu2024retrieval}, successor \citep{gould2023successor}---supply the
component vocabulary that a wiring-level version of this account would use.

\subsection{Implications}
\paragraph{Conditional computation has a second knob.}
Adaptive-depth and early-exit methods
\citep{graves2016adaptive,elbayad2020depth,schuster2022confident,raposo2024mixture}
adapt one scalar: how deep to go. If parallel demand is carried by heads and
serial demand by layers, then \emph{width} is an equally principled axis for
conditional computation, and $\gamma$ estimates how much width a given parallel
load requires. A demand-aware model would taper both.

\paragraph{Capability is predictable from shape, not only size.}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dmax} says the maximum serial depth a model can execute in one
forward pass is governed by $L_{\text{eff}}/c$. Two models with equal parameter
counts but different aspect ratios should therefore differ systematically in
serial-task competence (prediction~A4)---capability tied to architectural
\emph{shape} rather than scale.

\paragraph{Where chain-of-thought enters.}
If serial depth beyond the in-context budget must be externalised, the natural
continuation of the depth axis is across \emph{tokens}: past $D_{\max}$, serial
computation should migrate from layers to chain-of-thought steps, each running
a bounded-depth circuit \citep{merrill2024cot,li2024chain}. Measuring that
migration---serial structure moving from the depth axis to the token axis at
the ceiling---is the most direct extension of this work and we leave it to
follow-up.

\section{Limitations}
% ======================================================================
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Data recovered from figures.} Counts are recovered from rendered
bar charts (Section~\ref{sec:method}); they are validated to $\pm1$--$2$ heads
but are not the exact arrays. Regenerating numeric data from the pipeline is
the first roadmap item.
\item \textbf{Small models are noisy.} Pythia-70M/160M have few heads, so
their counts are quantisation-noisy and some rungs are non-monotone
(Table~\ref{tab:dc-total}); the clean trends rest on the larger models.
\item \textbf{Single threshold.} ``Dead'' is defined at $\tau=0.15$; the
robustness of the trend to $\tau$ must be demonstrated.
\item \textbf{Width and reasoning depth are not separated.} The
\texttt{deep\_chains} ladder raises reasoning depth and, incidentally, the
number of intermediate entities to hold; a width-only control is required to
attribute the vertical axis to demand and the horizontal axis to serial depth
specifically.
\item \textbf{The horizontal axis rests partly on recovered data.} The
frontier correlations are strongest and cleanest on the exact Llama-3-8B data
($r{=}{+}0.96$) but modest on individual figure-recovered models; the pooled
estimate ($r{=}{+}0.70$) and the exact check are what carry it. It should be
reproduced on exact data across the full sweep.
\item \textbf{No behavioural correlate yet.} We have not tied either axis to
task accuracy; the prediction is that both recruitment and frontier advance
track whether the model can actually perform the task, and that the frontier
plateau coincides with the accuracy collapse.
\item \textbf{Single prompt per condition.} No paraphrase variance is
reported, so surface-length confounds are not yet excluded. With one prompt per
$(D,W)$ cell, the coefficients of Eq.~\eqref{eq:law} are point estimates
without intervals and should not be quoted as measured exchange rates.
\item \textbf{Node activity is a marginal of the circuit.} Our observable
constrains a circuit's support and geometry but not its wiring
(Section~\ref{sec:circuits}); all structural claims are therefore about
\emph{shape}, not connectivity.
\item \textbf{Heads may not be the right parallel unit.} Attention heads are
polysemantic and features are superposed, so ``one parallel item $\approx\gamma$
heads'' may be diffuse rather than crisp; a feature-level version of $\Wh$ could
behave differently.
\item \textbf{Cross-architecture claims are predictions, not results.}
A1--A5 (Section~\ref{sec:arch}) follow from the framework but are untested
here: no MoE or state-space model was measured, and the GQA observation is
confounded with scale.
\item \textbf{Correlational, not causal.} $c$ and $\gamma$ describe allocation;
only ablation shows the recruited units are \emph{necessary}
(roadmap item~D$'$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Roadmap: experiments to finish the paper}\label{sec:roadmap}
% ======================================================================
The following is an ordered, concrete plan to convert these observations into
a strong submission.

\paragraph{A. Regenerate exact numeric data (removes Limitation~1).}
Add a \texttt{--dump-dead-matrix} option to the analysis pipeline that writes
the full per-$(\ell,h)$ attribution and the binary dead mask to CSV/JSON for
every (model, task), and rerun the six-model sweep. All tables in this paper
should then be regenerated from exact arrays.

\paragraph{B. Dissociate recruitment from reasoning depth (the key control).}
Add a \emph{width-control} suite that raises parallel demand at fixed
reasoning depth---e.g.\ retrieve $k$ \emph{independent}, non-chained facts in
one prompt for $k=1\ldots6$. Prediction: dead-head count falls with $k$ (more
heads recruited) even though no additional composition step is added. This is
the experiment that establishes recruitment as a demand response rather than a
depth artefact.

\paragraph{C. Tie both axes to behaviour.}
Score every model on every rung of every ladder (exact-match accuracy on the
final entity). Test two predictions: (i) recruitment co-varies with
success---where a model cannot perform the task, recruitment stalls; and
(ii) the \emph{active frontier plateaus at the depth where accuracy
collapses}, making the frontier a read-out of the model's usable serial depth.
Report, per model, the correlation of accuracy with both dead-head count and
frontier depth $q_{25}$.

\paragraph{C$'$. Confirm the horizontal axis on exact data across the sweep.}
The frontier effect is clean on exact Llama-3-8B attribution but rests on
pooled/recovered estimates for the GPT-2/Pythia models. Recompute $q_{25}$,
the centroid, and early-layer dead counts from the exact masks of item~A for
all models and rungs, and report per-model $r(\text{hops},q_{25})$ with
confidence intervals. This converts the headline two-axis result from
``suggestive across models, exact on one'' to ``exact across all.''

\paragraph{D$'$. Faithfulness: validate that ``active'' means ``necessary''.}
On models small enough for causal analysis (GPT-2 small/medium with
TransformerLens), ablate the heads we call dead and verify behaviour is
preserved, then ablate the active set and verify it breaks---the
faithfulness--sparsity curve of \citet{conmy2023towards}, using activation or
attribution patching \citep{nanda2023attribution,syed2023attribution} against a
proper corrupted baseline and reporting faithfulness \emph{and} completeness
\citep{hanna2024faithfulness,makelov2024illusions}. This single experiment
does three jobs: it makes the active set causal rather than correlational, it
\emph{defines} $\tau$ operationally (the elbow of the curve), and it licenses
the support claim of Section~\ref{sec:circuits}. It is a prerequisite for the
structural language used throughout.

\paragraph{D. Threshold robustness.}
Recompute all results for $\tau\in\{0.05,0.10,0.15,0.20\}$ and show the
recruitment trend (sign and approximate magnitude) is invariant. Include as a
supplementary figure.

\paragraph{E. Paraphrase and statistical rigour.}
Generate $\geq5$ surface paraphrases per condition (renamed entities, reordered
clauses, matched token length) and report means with confidence intervals and a
per-model Spearman $\rho$(demand, dead-count). This rules out length/surface
confounds and quantifies significance.

\paragraph{F. Extend to instruction-tuned and larger models.}
Repeat the cleanest ladder (\texttt{deep\_chains}, width-control) on at least
one modern model family (e.g.\ a Llama-3 or Pythia-1.4B/2.8B series) to test
whether the scale-dilation trend continues; restoring per-head attribution on
the large-model path is a prerequisite.

\paragraph{G. Head-identity analysis (optional, strengthens mechanism).}
Track \emph{which} heads are recruited as demand rises---are they consistent
across prompts, do early-recruited heads correspond to known functional types?
This connects the aggregate count to the circuits literature.

\paragraph{H. Two headline figures.}
(i) A small-multiples panel of $\dl$ for all six models $\times$ six rungs,
with the conserved sparse readout region shaded, so the recruitment-and-scale
story is legible at a glance. (ii) A two-axis summary: per model, total dead
(vertical) and frontier depth $q_{25}$ (horizontal) as functions of hop count,
so the coupled deformation and its scale dependence are visible together.

\paragraph{Deliverables checklist.}
exact-data regeneration (A) $\to$ width control (B) $\to$ behaviour coupling
(C) $\to$ exact two-axis confirmation (C$'$) $\to$ robustness + statistics
(D, E) $\to$ scale extension (F) $\to$ optional mechanism (G) $\to$ headline
figures (H). Items A--E and C$'$ are necessary for a credible submission;
F--H strengthen it.

% ======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ======================================================================
We introduced the dead-head landscape as a graded probe of how transformers
allocate their attention machinery to a task, and established a two-axis
demand--geometry law. As task demand rises the landscape deforms in two
coupled ways: it \emph{deflates}---models monotonically engage dormant heads,
drawing on a reserve whose size scales with capacity (vertical
recruitment)---and its \emph{active frontier advances}---each additional
reasoning step pulls computation deeper into the network at an estimable
per-hop layer cost (horizontal extension). Both effects are gated by spare
capacity, emerging in larger models and washing out in the smallest. The full
dataset and a concrete experimental roadmap are provided so the result can be
completed and validated.
